<Lead>
This tab hosts a working reference collection of rhetoric: classical and
modern figures of speech, concepts, movements, institutions, and biographies
of major rhetoricians, organized alphabetically from A to Z.
</Lead>

## How the entries are organized

Entries are grouped into letter sections in the sidebar. Each entry page
follows one consistent editorial template so that you can scan and compare
terms quickly:

- **Lead** — a one-paragraph definition of the term or profile of the figure.
- **Thematic sections** — the body, broken into short headed sections instead
  of unbroken blocks of text.
- **Examples and quotations** — primary-source quotations are set off as
  blockquotes and kept short.
- **Sources** — the bibliography behind the entry, with citations cleaned up
  and normalized.
- **Provenance** — a collapsible note crediting the original contributor whose
  entry the page adapts.

## Editorial standard

Source entries arrive as unbroken walls of prose, with hard line breaks,
broken words, and API artifacts from the export. Every page on this site is
therefore **rewritten, not copied**: the material is restated in contemporary
academic English, reorganized under headings, and formatted with callouts,
quotes, and lists. Factual content and the original contributors' credits are
preserved; the prose is not.
