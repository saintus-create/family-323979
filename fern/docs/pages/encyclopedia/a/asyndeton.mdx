title: "Asyndeton"
subtitle: "Omitting the expected conjunctions"
---

<Lead>
The omission of expected conjunctions between words, phrases, or clauses —
delivery without connective tissue.
</Lead>
<Badge intent="note">Figure of Speech</Badge>

## Three levels of omission

Spinoza omits the conjunction between **words** in describing peace as
a "disposition for benevolence, confidence, justice." Lincoln omits it
between **phrases**:

> The government of the people, by the people, for the people shall not
> perish from this earth.

And in Caesar's famous declaration — "I came, I saw, I conquered" — the
omission occurs between complete **clauses**.

## What the omission accomplishes

Asyndeton can speed up the sentence, as in the Caesar example, or it can
suggest the essential unity of the items, as in the Lincoln and Spinoza
examples. Omitting conjunctions can also evoke a sense of disorder, as
Marie-Claire Blais demonstrates:

> There had been so many funerals since grandmother Antoinette reigned over
> her household, little black deaths, in winter, disappearances of children,
> of babies, who had only lived a few months, mysterious disappearances of
> adolescents in autumn, in spring. (*Une Saison dans la vie d'Emmanuel*, 28)
