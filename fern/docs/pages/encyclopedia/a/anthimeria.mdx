<Lead>
The substitution of one part of speech for another — a form of
[Enallage](/e/enallage) — so that nouns act as verbs, adjectives as adverbs,
and so on.
</Lead>
<Badge intent="note">Figure of Speech</Badge>

## Shakespeare's coinages

Anthimeria lies behind many of Shakespeare's most memorable lines:

> Report that I am sudden sick. Quick and return! (*Antony and Cleopatra*,
> 1.3.4)

> I'll unhair thy head! (*Antony and Cleopatra*, 2.5.64)

> His complexion is perfect gallows. (*The Tempest*, 1.1.32)

In these lines Shakespeare uses an adjective as an adverb, a noun as a verb,
and a noun as an adjective.

## Modern poetry

Many notable lines of modern poetry are likewise the result of anthimeria:

> The hot of him is purest in the heart. (Stevens)

> he sang his didn't he danced his did. (Cummings)

> O dark dark dark. They all go into the dark. / The vacant interstellar
> spaces, the vacant into the vacant. (Eliot)
