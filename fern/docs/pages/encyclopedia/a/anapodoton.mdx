---
title: "Anapodoton"
subtitle: "Leaving the expected second clause unstated"
---

<Lead>
Also called *anantapodoton*: a kind of ellipsis in which an entire clause —
usually the second member of a correlative expression — is left unstated,
because the reader can supply it.
</Lead>
<Badge intent="note">Figure of Speech</Badge>


## Examples

When Jefferson writes:

> That to secure rights, Governments are instituted among Men, deriving
> their just powers from the governed,

it is obvious from what he has already written that he intends the reader to
supply "We hold self-evident . . ."

Likewise, when Caligula states, "Would that the Roman people had a single
neck," the unstated concluding clause is self-evident.
